For the nine-month period April through December, cumulative revenue is ₹5,243 crore, which puts us on a trajectory to close FY2025 at approximately ₹6,900 to ₹7,000 crore against the full-year target of ₹7,200 crore.

### AGENDA ITEM 2: RAW MATERIAL OUTLOOK AND MARGIN STRATEGY

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** Moving to item 2. The raw material basket for Coronet Paints has four major components: titanium dioxide — 28% of raw material cost; resins, primarily alkyd and acrylic — 34%; solvents — 16%; and additives, pigments, and extenders — 22%. TiO2 is the most volatile and the most watched. The current spot price for TiO2 rutile grade is approximately $3,250 per metric tonne, up from $2,900 in October 2024. Our contracted price through March is $3,050.

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** I want to add operational context here. We consume approximately 28,000 metric tonnes of TiO2 annually. Every $100 per metric tonne movement in TiO2 price translates to roughly ₹24 crore in annual cost impact at current volumes. If TiO2 moves to $3,500 — which some analysts are projecting by mid-calendar year 2025 — we are looking at a ₹108 crore annualized headwind versus our current contracted cost.

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** This is why the pricing action conversation cannot be deferred. We took a 2.5% price increase in August 2024. We need to take another increase, I believe in the range of 3 to 4%, effective April 1st.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Rakesh, I have to push back on the April timing. We are in the middle of the dealer incentive season. Q4 is our peak volume quarter — January through March is when contractors finish their projects before the monsoon and repaint demand is highest. If we announce a price increase in April, dealers will front-load in March, which gives us a volume bump in Q4, but we risk channel inventory overhang going into Q1 FY2026. And in the current competitive environment with Birla Opus and JSW Paints both making noise in the mid-tier segment, a price increase that is not matched by competition will cost us volume.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** Has competition indicated any pricing intent?

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Not formally. Asian Paints has been quiet on pricing since their November increase. Berger is watching. Birla Opus has not taken any meaningful price action — they're still in volume-building mode and they're not going to increase prices while they're trying to grab share.

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** Then we have a problem. If we increase and they don't match, we lose ground in the very segments we're already bleeding. But if we don't increase, our margins take a hit in H1 FY2026.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** Is there a middle path? Selectively increasing in premium and luxury segments where we have stronger pricing power, while holding in the economy segment?

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** That is actually what I was going to propose. Our premium range — Coronet Royale and Coronet Sheen — commands a 60 to 70% premium per litre over economy emulsions. The customer buying Royale is not price-sensitive in the same way. We can take a 4 to 5% increase in premium without losing volume. The economy range, we hold — or we reformulate. Devraj, can you speak to the reformulation option?

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** We have been working on a reformulated economy emulsion — internally called Project Slim — that reduces TiO2 loading by 18% through a combination of higher opacity extenders and improved dispersant technology. We get approximately 92% of the covering power at 82% of the TiO2 cost. The reformulation has passed internal quality testing. We have not yet done a market validation — whether end users perceive the quality difference.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** If the covering power is 92% of the original — will that eventually show up as a quality complaint?

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** At the economy price point, consumers are painting walls that require two coats regardless. The 8% reduction in single-coat opacity is not perceptible in a standard two-coat application. The issue would only arise if someone is doing a single coat repaint over a light existing color. It is a risk, but in our consumer research — we ran a blind panel test in September — only 3 out of 40 participants rated the reformulated product lower than the original.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** I'm comfortable with the reformulation data. I want to make sure we are not compromising on washability and sheen retention, which are the primary reasons consumers complain about economy paint.

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** Washability is unaffected — it is a function of the resin binder, not TiO2 loading. Sheen retention is also maintained.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Then the direction is: 4% price increase in Coronet Royale and Coronet Sheen, effective April 1st. Economy and mid-tier pricing held. Project Slim reformulation piloted in three markets — I'd suggest MP, Rajasthan, and Odisha — from April. Is that a resolution the board can support?

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** Yes, subject to monitoring how competition reacts in April. If Asian Paints takes an increase, we go broader.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Agreed.

**PALLAVI RAO:** Resolution recorded: 4% price increase in premium range effective April 1, 2025. Economy segment pricing held. Project Slim to be market-piloted in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Odisha from April 1, 2025, with a review by the MD at the August board meeting.

### AGENDA ITEM 3: COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Ashok, you have the floor.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Thank you. I want to give the board a clear picture of where we stand in the competitive map, because I think we have been slightly complacent. Coronet Paints holds an overall decorative paint market share of approximately 8.4% by volume as of the last Nielsen quarterly — down from 9.1% two years ago. We are the number four player nationally. Asian Paints at 38%, Berger at 17%, Kansai Nerolac at 11%, then us. That positioning is known. What is new in the last eighteen months is the emergence of two well-funded entrants.

Birla Opus — backed by the Aditya Birla Group — has committed ₹10,000 crore to paint manufacturing in India. They have already set up plants in Cheyyar, Tamil Nadu and Panipat, Haryana. They are playing a dealer-margin game — offering 5 to 8% higher margin than us at the retail counter. Dealers are rational people. If the product quality is comparable and the margin is higher, they push Birla. We have lost — and I want to be honest here — we have lost approximately 340 dealers in UP, Bihar, and West Bengal to Birla Opus in the last nine months. Not lost entirely, but they have reduced our SKU facing and added Birla as a primary brand.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** 340 dealers. That is a significant number.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** It is. And JSW Paints — which entered the market in 2019 but has been quiet — they have recently announced a second plant and a renewed push in the south. They have been strong in Karnataka and Andhra and they are now moving into Kerala, which is Kochi territory — which is our home market in many ways.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** Ashok, what is our response? I don't want to hear only the problem.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** The response has three parts. First — dealer economics. We are launching a revised dealer incentive program in February, which I'm calling the Coronet Partner Advantage scheme. We are increasing our base margin to dealers by 1.5%, which brings us to parity with what Birla is offering in most markets. Additionally, we are rolling out the Coronet Pro tinting machine — the newer model — to 2,500 dealer outlets by March 31st. The tinting machine creates stickiness that pure-price competition cannot replicate. A dealer with our machine has our entire color range available on demand. He has no incentive to switch because the machine is ours and we service it.

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** The tinting machine rollout is the most important competitive lever we have. I want to make sure we have the operational bandwidth to support it. Devraj?

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** We have 1,800 machines in the current network. The additional 700 have been ordered from our vendor in Ahmedabad. Delivery is confirmed for February 15th, and our field service team is being expanded — we are hiring 45 service engineers specifically for the tinting machine support network. This is already in the approved budget.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Second response — product. We have a gap in the waterproofing segment. Waterproofing is growing at 16 to 18% annually, significantly faster than decorative emulsions which are growing at 8 to 9%. Dr. Ferro and Sika dominate construction waterproofing, and Asian Paints has SmartCare. We have a waterproofing line but it is underdeveloped — three SKUs versus Asian Paints' twenty-plus. Vikram, I'll ask you to speak to the new product pipeline when you present on item 4 and 5.

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR (Head of Strategy — invited for this item):** Yes, I'll cover the waterproofing portfolio expansion in my section.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** Third response — geographies. We are overindexed to Maharashtra, Gujarat, and the south, and underindexed in the north and east. Specifically Uttar Pradesh — which is the largest paint market by state — we have 4.8% share versus our national average of 8.4%. The northern weakness is not a new problem but the entry of Birla Opus with their Panipat plant — which gives them logistics advantage in the north — makes it more urgent. I am proposing we establish a dedicated northern India task force, operating out of Lucknow, with separate P&L accountability. This would require board approval for the incremental headcount and the working capital commitment.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** How many people and what is the working capital requirement?

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** The task force — 28 additional sales and merchandising staff. Annual cost approximately ₹12 crore. Working capital for enhanced north India inventory positioning — ₹35 crore. ### AGENDA ITEM 4: CAPEX PROPOSAL — RAJASTHAN GREENFIELD PLANT

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Vikram, this is your item.

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** Thank you, Chairman. Good morning, board. I'll be focused on the Rajasthan proposal and then briefly touch on waterproofing. The strategic rationale for a new plant in Rajasthan — specifically the Neemrana Industrial Cluster in Alwar district — is threefold. One: proximity to the northern India market we just discussed. Our nearest existing plant is in Vapi, Gujarat. Logistics costs to serve UP and Rajasthan from Vapi add ₹8 to ₹12 per litre to our cost of sales in those markets versus a locally produced product. With a Neemrana plant, we eliminate that cost disadvantage entirely. Two: capacity. Our overall utilization across the four existing plants — Vapi, Hosur, Hyderabad, and Bhubaneswar — is running at 82% as of December 2024. That leaves limited headroom for volume growth. A new plant adds 1.2 lakh kilolitres of annual capacity, which supports the volume we need to reach ₹10,000 crore revenue — our FY2028 target. Three: Rajasthan government incentives. The state is offering a 25% capital subsidy on plant and machinery for paint and coatings manufacturing under their Rajasthan Investment Promotion Scheme 2024. We have received a Letter of Intent from the RIICO — Rajasthan Industrial Investment Corporation — dated December 20, 2024.

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** The subsidy is real and it changes the project economics significantly. Total project cost as estimated by our project team: ₹740 crore. With the 25% capital subsidy on eligible equipment — approximately ₹480 crore of that — the effective cash outflow is ₹620 crore net. The IRR on the project over a ten-year horizon, assuming 85% capacity utilization by Year 4 and current EBITDA margins, is 16.4%. Payback period: 6.2 years.

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** I want to add the operational design note. The Neemrana plant will be our most modern facility. We are designing it as a low-VOC focused plant — all water-based production lines, no solvent-based capacity. This is a deliberate choice. Solvent-based paints are in structural decline — BIS regulations are tightening, urban consumer preference is shifting, and several states are likely to impose VOC caps in the next three to five years. Building a future-proofed plant now is the right call.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** ₹740 crore is a major commitment. What is the phasing of the capex?

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** Year 1 — ₹220 crore for land, civil construction, and long-lead equipment orders. Year 2 — ₹350 crore for plant and machinery installation, utilities, and commissioning. Year 3 — ₹170 crore for remaining equipment, trial production, and ramp-up working capital. The plant would be operational in Q1 FY2027 — so approximately twenty-four months from financial close.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Funding. How are we proposing to fund this?

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** Internal accruals for ₹400 crore over the two-year period — we generate roughly ₹550 to ₹600 crore of operating cash annually and the capex is manageable alongside our ongoing maintenance capex of ₹80 crore per year. The balance ₹340 crore — net of the subsidy inflow — we are proposing a term loan. We have a preliminary term sheet from SBI at 7.85% floating, 10-year tenure, with a three-year moratorium. Final interest cost is comfortable within our debt servicing capacity. Our current net debt is essentially zero — we have net cash of ₹210 crore.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** I want to ask about environmental approvals. Rajasthan has had some delays in industrial clearances — what is our timeline assumption?

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** We have already engaged an environmental consultant — ERM India — and the preliminary environmental assessment is underway. The Neemrana cluster is a designated industrial zone, which simplifies approvals considerably. Our consultant estimates a twelve-month clearance timeline from application, which we plan to file in February 2025. If approvals run to fourteen or sixteen months, it delays the plant by a quarter or two but does not fundamentally change the project economics.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** Has the board reviewed the project feasibility report independently? I saw the management presentation but I want to confirm the independent technical review was done.

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** Yes. We engaged CRISIL Infrastructure Advisory for an independent review. Their report is in the board papers — pages 47 to 83. They have affirmed the project IRR within a range of 14.8% to 17.1% under different demand scenarios. The base case of 16.4% sits in the middle of their range.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** I'm satisfied. I support the approval.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Any objections to approving the Neemrana plant project?

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** No objections. In fact, from a sales perspective, I'd say the Neemrana plant cannot come soon enough. We need that northern capacity.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Very well. The Neemrana Greenfield Plant project is approved at a total project cost not exceeding ₹780 crore — I'm adding ₹40 crore contingency buffer to the ₹740 crore estimate. The MD and CFO are authorized to finalize land acquisition, sign the RIICO agreement, appoint project management consultants, and draw down the SBI term loan. Major procurement contracts above ₹50 crore to be presented to the board for ratification.

**PALLAVI RAO:** Neemrana Greenfield Plant approved. Project cost ceiling: ₹780 crore including contingency. MD and CFO authorized to execute agreements. Procurement above ₹50 crore to require board ratification.

### AGENDA ITEM 5: INTERNATIONAL EXPANSION — SRI LANKA AND BANGLADESH

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** Thank you. Coronet Paints currently exports to Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Nepal, and the Maldives through trading arrangements — we sell to local distributors who then market under their own brand or a Coronet sub-brand. Total export revenue in FY2024 was ₹187 crore, which is about 2.6% of our revenue. The proposal today is to move from a trading model to a formal market entry in Sri Lanka and Bangladesh — establishing our own subsidiary or joint venture, taking control of distribution, investing in brand building, and eventually looking at local manufacturing.

Sri Lanka first. The Sri Lankan paint market is approximately LKR 45 billion — roughly ₹1,200 crore equivalent — and is growing at 11% annually as the economy recovers from the 2022-23 crisis. The major players are Nippon Paint, Akzo Nobel, and two local brands — Rocell Paints and Lankem. There is no dominant Indian brand. Our existing distributor in Colombo — Premier Coats Lanka — has built modest but real Coronet presence, particularly in Kandy and the Central Province. We have 6.2% volume share in the market segments where we are present, which is better than we expected for a trading arrangement.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** The Sri Lanka opportunity is real. I visited Colombo in November and I met the Premier Coats team. Their operation is modest — eleven salespeople, one warehouse — but the brand goodwill they have built for Coronet in the professional contractor segment is impressive. If we put real marketing and distribution investment behind it, I believe 12 to 15% market share in decorative is achievable in five years.

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** The proposal for Sri Lanka is to acquire a 51% stake in Premier Coats Lanka — they are open to this — for an estimated consideration of LKR 280 million, approximately ₹7.8 crore at current exchange. We then inject ₹25 crore over three years for marketing, team building, and working capital. The total Sri Lanka commitment is approximately ₹33 crore over three years. The risk is contained. The upside is building a foundation in a market that will grow.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** The LKR has been volatile since 2022. How are we managing currency risk in the Sri Lanka investment?

**MS. TARA BHOSALE:** Good question. The investment is denominated in LKR, so the rupee value of the acquisition cost moves with the currency. We are not hedging the equity investment — it is not standard practice to hedge equity. However, receivables from our current export sales to Sri Lanka, which are USD-denominated, are hedged on a quarterly rolling basis. Going forward, as we internalize the Sri Lanka entity, most transactions will be local-currency and exposure will be naturally contained.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** I want to spend a moment on Bangladesh. That is a larger and more complex market.

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** Bangladesh paint market is approximately BDT 6,200 crore — roughly ₹4,600 crore equivalent. It is one of the fastest-growing paint markets in the region, driven by rapid urbanization and a large real estate construction cycle. Berger Paints Bangladesh — which is a separately listed entity — is the dominant player with approximately 40% market share. Asian Paints has a small presence. There are several large local players.

The Bangladesh political environment has changed significantly since August 2024. The Sheikh Hasina government's departure has created uncertainty — but also opportunity. Several Indian companies that had delayed Bangladesh investments due to regulatory friction are now finding the interim government more receptive to foreign direct investment. We have been in discussions with a Dhaka-based construction materials group — Bashundhara Group — about a potential JV for paint manufacturing and distribution. They bring land, local network, and regulatory navigation. We bring product, brand, and technology.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** What is the financial profile of a Bangladesh JV?

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** The indicative term is a 50-50 JV. Coronet's equity contribution — for a 50,000 kilolitre plant and initial working capital — would be in the range of ₹90 to ₹110 crore. The JV would be set up as a Bangladeshi entity under the FDI framework. Revenue potential in Year 5, assuming 8 to 10% market share: BDT 650 to 700 crore, approximately ₹480 to ₹520 crore equivalent. EBITDA at 18% margin would be ₹86 to ₹94 crore at that scale.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** The Bangladesh political risk cannot be ignored. The transition is uncertain. Bashundhara Group — how politically connected are they to the previous regime?

**MR. VIKRAM NAIR:** It is a fair question. Bashundhara has historically had close government ties but they are a private-sector industrial group, not a political entity. They have maintained operational continuity through the transition. However, I want to be transparent — we have not completed our political risk assessment for Bangladesh and I do not think the board should approve an irreversible commitment today. What I am asking for today is approval to continue detailed due diligence — political risk assessment, legal review of the JV structure, financial due diligence on Bashundhara — at a cost not exceeding ₹3 crore, and to return to the board by June 2025 with a go/no-go recommendation.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** That is the right approach for Bangladesh. Sri Lanka — I think we are comfortable proceeding. Any dissent?

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** No dissent. Sri Lanka is a clear yes.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** Sri Lanka — yes. Bangladesh — agree with Vikram, due diligence first.

**PALLAVI RAO:** Two resolutions recorded. Resolution 1: Sri Lanka — MD authorized to negotiate acquisition of 51% stake in Premier Coats Lanka. Investment envelope of ₹33 crore over three years. Final terms to be approved by MD and CFO, with board notification. Resolution 2: Bangladesh — due diligence approved, budget not exceeding ₹3 crore, to be completed by May 31, 2025. Go/no-go recommendation to be presented to the board by June 30, 2025.

### AGENDA ITEM 7: INVESTOR RELATIONS — ANALYST GUIDANCE

Street consensus for Coronet Paints FY2025 revenue is currently ₹7,050 crore — our internal tracking suggests we will likely come in at ₹6,950 to ₹7,000 crore. That is a modest miss versus consensus, and I expect we will have some earnings estimate downgrades after the Q3 results are published. The good news is that the margin story — 270 basis points of EBITDA expansion in Q3 — is stronger than consensus was expecting. Most analysts modeled 150 to 180 basis points. So there is a positive surprise on the quality of earnings even if topline is slightly light.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** What are analysts most focused on for FY2026 guidance?

**MS. LEENA SHETTY:** Three things. First, raw material trajectory — specifically TiO2 — and how we are managing it. Second, the competitive response to Birla Opus and JSW. Third, growth drivers — specifically whether there is a new capacity expansion story. The Neemrana announcement will be very well received. Analysts have been asking when Coronet would announce capacity expansion for two quarters now.

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** On guidance — I am comfortable saying FY2026 revenue target is ₹7,800 to ₹8,200 crore, implying 12 to 16% growth. I am not comfortable guiding on EBITDA margin for FY2026 given the raw material uncertainty.

### AGENDA ITEM 8: ANY OTHER BUSINESS

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Any other business?

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** One item I want to flag briefly — ESG. We have committed in our Annual Report to reducing our Scope 1 and Scope 2 carbon emissions by 30% by FY2030 versus a FY2022 baseline. We are currently at a 9% reduction after two years. The pace needs to accelerate. I would like the board's endorsement to include ESG capex — specifically solar panel installation at our Vapi and Hosur plants — in the ₹80 crore annual maintenance capex envelope rather than requiring separate board approval each time, as long as individual projects are under ₹15 crore.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** That seems reasonable. Any objection?

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** No objection. In fact, I'd suggest we ask Devraj to present a comprehensive ESG roadmap at the April board meeting. The 9% reduction in two years against a 30% target by FY2030 means we need to average 3.5% per year from here. That pace is achievable but not automatic.

**MR. DEVRAJ CHOUDHARY:** Agreed. I'll have an ESG roadmap ready for April.

**PALLAVI RAO:** ESG capex up to ₹15 crore per project included in maintenance capex envelope, within the ₹80 crore annual limit. Comprehensive ESG roadmap to be presented at the April 2025 board meeting.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** I want to raise something slightly uncomfortable. The board has discussed the competitive challenge from Birla Opus at length today. Birla Opus has deep pockets — the Aditya Birla Group has essentially unlimited financial firepower relative to Coronet. In a sustained price war or margin squeeze, they can outlast us. I want the board to think — not decide today, but think — about whether Coronet's long-term strategic position is as an independent entity or as part of a larger group. I am not suggesting we sell. I am suggesting we should have a view on this before someone else forces that conversation.

*[Pause. Several board members are visibly surprised by this comment.]*

**MR. RAKESH SINGHANIA:** Cyrus, that is a significant statement. I want to understand — are you suggesting we explore a merger or acquisition?

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** I am suggesting the board should have a private conversation about strategic options at the appropriate time. Nothing more. I raise it because it is better to have that conversation from a position of strength — ₹623 crore cash, improving margins, a growth story — than from a position of distress.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** I take the point in the spirit it is intended. I think the responsible thing to say is that the board's primary strategy is to compete and win as an independent entity. The Neemrana plant, the Sri Lanka and Bangladesh expansion, the north India push — these are the actions of a company that intends to remain independent and relevant. However, the board's fiduciary duty requires that we remain open to all options that serve shareholder value. I'd suggest Cyrus and I have a bilateral conversation about this in the next two weeks and we bring a structured perspective to the board — if and when that is warranted.

**MR. CYRUS IRANI:** That is all I was asking for. Thank you, Chairman.

**DR. SUNITA VERMA:** I want to note for the record that I think Cyrus raised an important point with appropriate nuance. The board should be proactive on strategic positioning. I support the bilateral conversation.

**MR. ASHOK PILLAI:** One very brief item. The Coronet Color Festival — our annual consumer event — has been running in Mumbai and Pune for five years. This year I want to take it to Lucknow and Jaipur as part of the north India push. Budget requirement: approximately ₹4 crore incremental. It's within the approved marketing budget and I don't technically need board approval, but I mention it because it aligns with our north India strategy and I want visibility.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Good initiative. Noted. Pallavi, no resolution required — it is within the approved opex budget.

**PALLAVI RAO:** Noted.

**MR. HARSHVARDHAN DALMIA:** Anything else? *(pause)* No? Then we close. Before I do — this was a productive meeting. We have approved the largest single capital investment in Coronet's history, entered a new international market, addressed the competitive challenge with a structured response, and declared a dividend.
