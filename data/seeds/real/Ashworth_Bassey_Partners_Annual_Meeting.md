### ITEM 6: THE HARGREAVES MORTON LLP ALLIANCE PROPOSAL

**MR. CHUKWUEMEKA BASSEY:** Everyone has read the proposal in their board pack. Hargreaves Morton is a mid-size London firm — 68 partners, 280 lawyers — with a strong Africa practice. They are not magic circle. But their Africa group has handled eleven Nigeria-related ICSID cases in the last six years, and they have a Francophone Africa desk that we have no equivalent for. They approached us in September 2024 through a mutual contact — Justice Okeke, retired Court of Appeal judge who serves on their advisory board.

Their proposal: a formal best-friend alliance. Exclusive referral arrangement for Nigerian matters. Joint pitching on cross-border mandates. Shared access to their know-how and precedent libraries. No merger — our independence is preserved. Client billings stay with us on Nigeria work, stay with them on UK and international work.

**MRS. FUNKE ADELEKE-OBI:** What do they get from this?

**MR. CHUKWUEMEKA BASSEY:** Access to our client relationships and local counsel capacity. And frankly, our reputation. Hargreaves Morton has had three Nigerian firms as their "best friend" in the last eight years. None of them lasted more than two years. Their Africa practice needs credibility in Nigeria and we give them that.

**MR. NNAMDI EJIFOR:** I have a concern about exclusivity. If we commit to exclusive referrals to Hargreaves Morton, and they cannot service a matter — conflict of interest, capacity, whatever — we are stuck. Our clients' needs cannot be hostage to their limitations.

**MR. CHUKWUEMEKA BASSEY:** That is a valid point and it is negotiable. The exclusivity clause in their draft is broad and we would push to narrow it to matters above a $1 million threshold and subject to a conflicts-check carve-out.

**MRS. ADAORA CHUKWUMA:** The real estate crossover is interesting to me. We are getting instructed on real estate matters that have an English law component — REIT structures, Cayman holding companies, Jersey SPVs. We are having to refer that work out ad hoc to firms we don't have strong relationships with. **MR. BABATUNDE OYELARAN:** My concern is different. Hargreaves Morton's technology practice — I looked at their website and their lateral hires — is stronger than ours. If we are in an alliance with them, what happens when a Nigerian tech company with a cross-border structure wants legal advice? Do we get the matter or does Hargreaves Morton capture it at the London end and never share it back?

**MR. CHUKWUEMEKA BASSEY:** That is exactly the governance question the alliance agreement needs to answer. Babatunde, would you be willing to review the draft agreement specifically for those provisions and come back to the partnership with a marked-up version by March 7th?

**MR. BABATUNDE OYELARAN:** Yes. And I'll want to speak to their technology partner directly before I give a view.

**MR. SEUN OLATUNJI:** Emeka, I support the alliance in principle. After the Petrolex experience, I cannot be the person who argues that we don't need international capacity. What I want is the governance to be right. If the governance is right, I'm in favor.

### ITEM 7: NEW PRACTICE GROUP — TECHNOLOGY, DATA & AI

*[Miss Uwannah and Mr. Dada rejoin for this item.]*

**MR. BABATUNDE OYELARAN:** I will be brief and then I want the two associates to speak, because this proposal is substantially theirs. The IP & Technology practice currently generates ₦40 million — 1% of firm revenue. That is not because technology law lacks demand. It is because we have been treating technology law as a subset of intellectual property — trademark registrations, software licensing, the occasional NITDA compliance matter. We are leaving an enormous market unserved.

In 2024, the Nigeria Data Protection Commission issued 86 enforcement actions. The National Information Technology Development Agency published new guidelines for AI systems used in financial services. Three Nigerian startup unicorn candidates — companies that are raising Series B and Series C funding — needed advice on data governance, algorithmic accountability, and cross-border data transfer restrictions, and they went to firms in London and Nairobi because there was no credible Nigerian firm that could advise on those questions at the level their investors required.

Chioma, can you explain the scope of what you've put together?

**MISS CHIOMA UWANNAH:** Thank you. Mr. Oyelaran asked me to develop a proposal for a dedicated Technology, Data and AI practice group. I worked with Olawale and two other associates — Ifunanya and Tunde — over four months. We conducted a market analysis. We identified twelve Nigerian companies that have had regulatory technology incidents in the last two years — data breaches, NDPC investigations, algorithmic decisions that generated regulatory scrutiny. None of those twelve companies had Nigerian external counsel advising them on the technology law dimension. They were using either in-house counsel with no specialist training or foreign firms.

The opportunity is real. Our proposal is: a dedicated practice group with six associates as its founding core — all of whom have expressed strong interest — supported by investment in specialist training, IAPP certification for data protection, and a targeted lateral hire of one technology law specialist at the senior associate or junior partner level.

Revenue projection for year one — which we have been conservative about: ₦180 million. By year three, if we position correctly: ₦600 million. That would make Technology our third-largest practice group by revenue.

**MR. TAYO FASHOLA:** How much does the investment cost?

**MR. BABATUNDE OYELARAN:** The training and certification program: ₦12 million. The lateral hire — a candidate I have identified, a senior associate at a London firm who wants to return to Nigeria — their expected compensation is ₦22 million per annum. Technology-specific research platforms and precedent libraries: ₦8 million. Total first-year investment: ₦42 million.

**MR. NNAMDI EJIFOR:** ₦42 million for a practice group with a Year 1 revenue target of ₦180 million. The payback is within the year, even on conservative assumptions.

**MR. SEUN OLATUNJI:** I support this. The Energy sector is going to generate technology-adjacent work — digital oilfield technology, energy data platforms, carbon credit registries. I would use a Technology practice group constantly.

**MRS. ADAORA CHUKWUMA:** Same for Real Estate — PropTech, digital land registry questions, smart building data. We need this.

**MR. CHUKWUEMEKA BASSEY:** Chioma, before you leave — this proposal is impressive. I want to put on record that the quality of the thinking here is the exact kind of contribution that the partnership will remember when consideration for seniority comes. That is not a promise. It is an observation.

**MISS CHIOMA UWANNAH:** Thank you, sir.

*[Uwannah and Dada leave.]*
