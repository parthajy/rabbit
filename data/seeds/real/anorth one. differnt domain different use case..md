Sure — here’s **another version in a different domain and use case**: a **hospital procurement meeting** about adopting a SaaS platform for **inventory, billing, patient support, and lab workflow tracking**. It has a different tone, different stakeholders, and a different operational problem.

***

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Let’s begin. We have forty-five minutes, and I’d prefer we use them to answer the real question: does this platform reduce operational chaos, or does it just give us another screen to stare at while the problems stay the same?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Fair start. I’m Arjun from procurement, and I’ll be direct about price, contract scope, and implementation risk.

**Priya Menon:** Priya, hospital administration. I care about whether this helps our staff move faster without creating another compliance headache.

**Suresh Iyer:** Suresh from finance. I’ll be looking at cost, billing accuracy, claims flow, and whether the numbers actually improve.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** Kavita, medical records. I want to know whether patient data, lab results, and admission records are easier to manage without breaking privacy rules.

**Mehul Shah:** Mehul, IT. My focus is integration, uptime, access controls, and support response time.

**Farah Khan:** Farah, nursing operations. I care about whether this reduces the time nurses spend chasing status updates and missing information.

**Rohit Bansal:** Rohit, lab operations. I want to know if lab requests, sample tracking, and result reporting can be cleaned up.

**Anita Das:** Anita, patient support. I handle complaints, discharge follow-ups, and family queries, so I care about visibility and response speed.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Good. We’re here because the hospital is reviewing a workflow platform that touches inventory, billing, support, and clinical admin. The current process is fragmented. Supplies are tracked in one system, billing in another, patient support in spreadsheets, and lab requests through email and phone. We need to decide whether this SaaS platform actually solves that.

**Arjun Malhotra:** The proposal is for a **₹27 lakh** annual platform, plus **₹6 lakh** implementation. That includes inventory workflow tracking, billing reconciliation, support ticket management, lab request routing, and reporting dashboards.

**Suresh Iyer:** That price is not tiny. What exactly are we replacing?

**Priya Menon:** Not replacing everything on day one. We’re connecting the workflows first. If the platform can reduce manual follow-up and billing errors, it may eventually let us retire one or two legacy tools.

**Mehul Shah:** Let me ask the obvious question. What systems does it integrate with?

**Arjun Malhotra:** The proposal says it connects with your billing software, patient support ticketing tool, lab request queue, and inventory register. We also need to review whether it can connect to your current reporting database.

**Mehul Shah:** “Need to review” is doing a lot of work there.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Fair. We have a technical discovery session scheduled for next week if you approve moving forward.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Before we talk discovery, I want to understand the operational pain. Farah, from nursing, what’s the biggest issue right now?

**Farah Khan:** Information delay. A patient is discharged, but nursing, billing, and patient support all get different versions of the discharge status at different times. That causes confusion, delays in finalization, and a lot of calls that should not be necessary.

**Anita Das:** And from patient support, we spend too much time asking other departments for basic updates. Families don’t care whose system is incomplete. They just want an answer.

**Rohit Bansal:** Lab has similar issues. A sample status changes, but the ward, doctor, and patient desk don’t always see it immediately. We lose time chasing confirmation.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** This is also a medical records issue. If the source of truth is fragmented, we risk inconsistent documentation.

**Suresh Iyer:** And finance absorbs the cost of all that confusion. Delayed billing, corrections, and rework are not free.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** So the problem is real. The question is whether the platform is the right answer. Arjun, what’s the implementation timeline?

**Arjun Malhotra:** If approved, discovery starts on **15 April 2026**, workflow mapping on **17 April 2026**, pilot launch on **22 April 2026**, and a review on **5 June 2026**. The pilot would cover a limited set of wards, billing cases, and lab workflows.

**Priya Menon:** That’s quite quick. Can the staff handle that much change?

**Arjun Malhotra:** The pilot would be narrow. We’re not asking the whole hospital to switch at once.

**Farah Khan:** That matters. If you try to retrain every unit at once, you’ll create rebellion.

**[A few people chuckle.]**

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Let’s talk use case by use case. Inventory first. Can it help with stock visibility and reorder alerts?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Yes. The platform can track inventory requests, thresholds, low-stock alerts, and approval routing. It can also show who requested what, when, and whether the request was approved.

**Suresh Iyer:** That could help reduce waste too. We’ve had expired stock sitting unnoticed in storage.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Exactly. Better visibility helps both availability and waste control.

**Rohit Bansal:** What about lab workflow?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Lab requests can be routed through the system, with status updates for collection, processing, and result release. The idea is to reduce phone calls and manual follow-ups.

**Rohit Bansal:** Good, but our lab has exceptions. Some tests are routine, some are urgent, and some need additional review. Can the workflow handle that?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Yes, but we’d need to define the rules during discovery. The platform is configurable.

**Mehul Shah:** Configurable is fine, but I need to know about support and uptime. If this becomes critical to hospital operations, downtime matters.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Standard support is included, with escalation for critical incidents. Uptime commitments are in the proposal, and we can review SLA terms in the revised commercial sheet.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** I’m concerned about patient privacy. Who can see what?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Role-based access. Different departments see different data. Audit logs track changes. Sensitive patient information can be limited to authorized staff.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** Good, because privacy failure is not a theoretical risk in a hospital.

**Anita Das:** From patient support, I need case histories, discharge notes, and complaint records in one view. Right now, staff bounce between systems and ask patients to repeat themselves.

**Arjun Malhotra:** That’s one of the main support workflow improvements. Cases can be linked to patient records, discharge status, and escalation history.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** What’s the price justification?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Reduced rework, faster billing, fewer missed updates, better inventory control, and less manual coordination. If the platform saves even a small amount of staff time across departments, it offsets part of the cost. More importantly, it reduces errors that lead to delays and complaints.

**Suresh Iyer:** I’m not convinced by “part of the cost.” Give me a number.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Fair. Based on current volumes, we estimate about **90 to 120 hours per month** of manual coordination could be reduced across administration, support, and lab operations. Billing corrections could also drop if workflow handoffs improve.

**Suresh Iyer:** That’s more concrete.

**Priya Menon:** I’d also like to point out that staff burnout has a cost even if it doesn’t show up in a spreadsheet.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** That’s true, but finance still needs a spreadsheet.

**[Laughter.]**

**Farah Khan:** What would the staff actually see? I don’t want something that looks fancy but is hard to use under pressure.

**Arjun Malhotra:** The interface is role-based. Nurses would see task status and patient workflow. Patient support would see case updates and discharge information. Lab teams would see request queues and status. Billing would see invoice-related workflow events.

**Farah Khan:** That sounds better than what we have now.

**Mehul Shah:** I still need technical proof, but I’m less worried now. The real risk is overcomplication. If the platform makes things more complex, adoption will fail.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Agreed. That’s why discovery is essential.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Let’s hear objections now, not later. What’s the biggest reason not to do this?

**Suresh Iyer:** From finance, the biggest objection is scope creep. A pilot becomes a project. A project becomes a dependency. Then we are paying for software and still using spreadsheets.

**Priya Menon:** From admin, the risk is staff resistance. If the workflow is not simple enough, people will avoid it.

**Mehul Shah:** From IT, the risk is integration maintenance. If every exception becomes a custom fix, we’ll own a new burden.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** From medical records, the risk is privacy and documentation discipline.

**Anita Das:** From patient support, the risk is incomplete information. If the system doesn’t give us the whole picture, it’s useless.

**Rohit Bansal:** From lab, the risk is that the system doesn’t understand urgency and exceptions.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** All fair. Arjun, what do you need from us to proceed?

**Arjun Malhotra:** Approval for technical discovery, permission to map workflow samples from billing, lab, support, and inventory, and a decision on whether you want a limited pilot or a larger phased rollout.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** I’m leaning pilot. But I want the pilot to be meaningful, not cosmetic.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Agreed.

**Mehul Shah:** And I want the SLA documentation before any commitment.

**Arjun Malhotra:** You’ll get it.

**Suresh Iyer:** Finance wants a revised commercial sheet with hidden costs clearly listed.

**Arjun Malhotra:** Done.

**Priya Menon:** If the pilot includes nursing and support, I’m in.

**Farah Khan:** Same.

**Anita Das:** Support should be included early.

**Rohit Bansal:** Lab too.

**Dr. Kavita Sen:** Medical records needs visibility in the pilot as well.

**Dr. Nandini Rao:** Then we have a path. Let’s summarize.
