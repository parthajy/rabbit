## PREFATORY NOTE *(Dr. Folake Adesanya, Registrar)*

This meeting represents the first formal gathering of the University's joint leadership and donor council since the Five-Year Strategic Plan was adopted on **March 15, 2020**. That plan — titled *Meridian 2025: Towards Research Excellence and Continental Impact* — set out specific commitments across four pillars: research output, student admissions and welfare, infrastructure, and institutional partnerships. This review assesses performance against those commitments, received the accounts of all four deans, and charts the next strategic cycle: *Meridian 2030*.

The 2020 meeting was held in person at the same venue, immediately before the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns were announced. Several trustees have noted the symbolic significance of returning to the Senate Chamber five years later.

### SESSION FOUR: FINANCIAL REVIEW

**MR. BABATUNDE OJO (Bursar):** Thank you. I will present the key financial metrics and then open for questions.

Total institutional revenue for FY2024: **₦7.84 billion.** This comprises tuition and fees — ₦4.92 billion (63%); endowment drawdown — ₦780 million (10%); research grants and contracts — ₦1.02 billion (13%); donor contributions received in-year — ₦620 million (8%); consultancy and commercial income — ₦310 million (4%); MeridianOnline fees — ₦210 million (3%).

Total expenditure FY2024: **₦7.21 billion.** Surplus: ₦630 million — the first operating surplus in the university's history above ₦500 million. Staff costs constitute 56% of expenditure — ₦4.04 billion. Academic staff payroll has increased 38% in naira terms over five years, but in real terms — adjusted for inflation — academic staff have effectively seen a wage reduction of approximately 12% since 2020. That is the honest number, and it explains Prof. Adebayo's retention issue.

**PROF. KUNLE ADEBAYO:** Thank you, Babatunde, for naming it directly. I want the donor council to understand what we are competing against. A senior lecturer at Meridian earns approximately ₦7.2 million per annum. The same profile at a Big Four consulting firm earns ₦18 to ₦24 million. A law firm partnership track offers ₦30 to ₦40 million. We are not competing with other universities for faculty — we are competing with the private sector, and we are losing. Between 2020 and 2024, we lost 31 academic staff to industry or to international universities. We hired 78 new faculty — so the net position is positive — but the ones we are losing are disproportionately the experienced, research-active faculty who take their networks and their students with them when they go.

**MR. FEMI ADEYINKA:** Is this fixable without dramatically increasing tuition?

**MR. BABATUNDE OJO:** Yes, but it requires endowment growth. Endowed chairs — where a donor funds a permanent salary supplement for a named professorship — are the global standard for this problem. A ₦500 million endowed chair, drawing down at 5% per year, generates ₦25 million annually — enough to bring a world-class professor to Meridian at a salary that is internationally competitive. We currently have two endowed chairs. We need twenty.

**ALHAJI SIR REGINALD OKONKWO:** Twenty endowed chairs. What would each one cost?

**MR. BABATUNDE OJO:** To endow a chair at the level where the drawdown covers a competitive professorial salary — ₦400 to ₦600 million per chair, depending on faculty and discipline.

**ALHAJI SIR REGINALD OKONKWO:** I will endow three. In the name of my wife, my father, and one in the name of this institution's future. That is ₦1.5 billion. Babatunde, speak to my office next week about the structure.

The difference between a donation that lives and a donation that dies is whether the institution knows what it is for. Meridian University, when I committed to it in 2008, was a piece of paper and a dream that Adebowale presented to me on a Sunday morning in my sitting room. What I see today is not that dream — it is larger than that dream. The third in Nigeria ranking. King's College Hospital recruiting our medical graduates. A malaria paper in *Nature Medicine*. These are not small things. These are serious things.

But I also want to say — and I want the deans to hear this — the next five years cannot be about numbers alone. The enrollment number, the ranking number, the endowment number. Numbers are evidence of something. What I want to see in 2030 is evidence that Meridian University has changed how Nigeria thinks. Changed how the continent approaches a problem. That a government somewhere made a better decision because of research that came from this campus. That is the ambition I want the Meridian 2030 plan to carry.

*[Silence. Several seconds.]*

**PROF. ADEBOWALE OGUNDIMU:** Alhaji. If that is the brief, it is the best brief I have ever been given.

### SESSION SIX: MERIDIAN 2030 — FRAMING THE NEXT STRATEGIC CYCLE

**PROF. ADEBOWALE OGUNDIMU:** I want to use the final session to frame, not finalize, the Meridian 2030 plan. The detailed plan will be developed between now and June 2025, in consultation with all four faculties, the student union, the NUC, and the donor council. What I want to confirm today are the strategic pillars.

Pillar One — **Research Impact.** The 2025 plan measured research by output — number of papers. The 2030 plan will measure by impact — citations, policy citations, patents, licensing income, and the number of research outputs that demonstrably influenced a policy, a product, or a practice. The target: 5 policy citations of Meridian research by Nigerian government agencies or international bodies by 2030. Three licensed technologies generating commercial revenue. Two research-led spin-off companies.

Pillar Two — **Admissions and Access.** Total enrollment target: **20,000 students by December 2029.** MeridianOnline: **15,000 learners.** Graduation rate: **88% minimum.** Scholarship coverage: **30% of students** on some form of financial support. Gender parity: **50:50 maintained.**

Pillar Three — **Faculty Excellence.** PhD qualification rate: **85% by 2030.** Endowed chairs: **twelve** — we have two, Alhaji has committed three more, leaving seven to be established by the 2030 deadline. International faculty: **15% of academic staff** from outside Nigeria.

Pillar Four — **Financial Sustainability.** Endowment: **$50 million equivalent** by December 2030. Non-tuition revenue as a share of total income: from 37% today to **50% by 2030.** This means growing research grants, commercialization income, and donor income so that tuition fees — and the price sensitivity that comes with them — are less dominant.

Pillar Five — **Continental Relevance.** Alhaji's brief. This is the hardest to measure and the most important. We will establish a Meridian Policy Lab — a dedicated research-to-policy unit — that works directly with government agencies, the African Union, and the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa to translate our research into actionable policy frameworks. We will establish a Visiting African Leaders in Residence program — bringing senior policymakers, scientists, and practitioners from across the continent to spend time on this campus. We will host the inaugural Meridian African Education Summit in 2027, which will bring together vice chancellors, ministers of education, and international funders to set an African higher education agenda that goes beyond rankings.

**DR. EMMANUEL BOATENG:** The Policy Lab and the African Education Summit — those are ideas I will personally advocate for at the African Union Education Commission. I sit on that body and the conversation about what African universities can do for African governance is one that needs a convener. Meridian should be that convener.

**MRS. PATRICIA ASHFORD:** The Visiting African Leaders program — is there a model you are drawing on?

**PROF. ADEBOWALE OGUNDIMU:** The Weatherhead Center at Harvard, somewhat. The African Leadership Centre at King's College London. We are not copying them — we are learning from them. The Meridian version will be African-led and African-funded, which is both a political statement and a practical necessity.

**ALHAJI SIR REGINALD OKONKWO:** Good. We do not need to inherit models. We can build our own.

**BARR. CHIAMAKA DIALA:** Let me summarize the commitments made today before we close.

From Alhaji Sir Reginald Okonkwo: Three endowed chairs at ₦500 million each — total ₦1.5 billion. To be structured as permanent endowments with named professorships.

From Mrs. Patricia Ashford CBE and the Ashford Trust: Continuation of existing scholarship and partnership facilitation funding, plus a new £300,000 Faculty Retention and Excellence Fund. Increased contribution to the Mental Health and Wellness Centre. Total UK commitment for the next cycle to be formalized by June 30, 2025.

**PROF. ADEBOWALE OGUNDIMU:** Total new donor commitments from today's meeting: approximately ₦3.15 billion in Nigerian naira equivalent, plus the sterling and dollar commitments. This is the largest single-day donor commitment in this institution's history.

That conviction has not wavered. What has grown is our evidence that the conviction is correct. The student who will change something important is somewhere in this country right now, wondering if there is a place for them. Our job is to make sure this is that place. Thank you all.
