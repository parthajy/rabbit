***

**Arjun Khanna:** Alright, everyone, let’s get started. It’s **12 April 2026**, we’re in the boardroom, and I’d like us to treat this as a working session rather than a ceremonial punishment. The quarter is not where we wanted it to be. We’re going to talk through why, what we can do now, and what we absolutely cannot afford to ignore. I want the conversation to be direct, not theatrical. We already have enough theatre in the numbers.

**Neha Malhotra:** Direct is good. I’m Neha from finance, and I’ll start with the part everyone already knows but nobody enjoys hearing out loud: Q1 closed below plan. Revenue landed at **₹14.2 crore** against a target of **₹18 crore**. Gross margin fell to **41%** from **48%** last quarter, and net burn was higher than budget by about **₹1.1 crore**.

**Kabir Sethi:** That’s the clean version, and even the clean version hurts. Kabir, operations. I can already tell this meeting is going to involve the words “discipline,” “process,” and “why is this taking so long.”

**Rhea Iyer:** Rhea, sales. I’ll say what the sales team is saying quietly in WhatsApp: the quarter was rough, pipeline conversion was weak, and we lost too many deals to delay, pricing, and competitor urgency.

**Vikram Menon:** Vikram, product. I know I’m going to be blamed for at least one thing, probably two, and maybe three if we’re being spiritually accurate.

**Shalini Bose:** Shalini from customer success. We lost a few renewals that we shouldn’t have lost, and I’m here to explain why, without pretending the issue was one customer’s “unreasonable expectations.”

**Ananya Roy:** Ananya, engineering. I’m prepared to discuss delivery, quality, incident response, and the fact that everyone thinks engineering can somehow compress three months of work into one week because the quarter is in trouble.

**Prakash Nair:** Prakash, HR and talent. I’ll be focused on attrition, morale, hiring delays, and the part where this quarter made several people look at LinkedIn like it was a survival tool.

**Maya Iyer:** Maya, marketing. I care about lead quality, campaign performance, and whether our messaging is landing or just being politely ignored by the market.

**Sandeep Rao:** Sandeep, customer support. I’m here because support tickets went up, escalations took too long, and half the team spent March sounding like they were apologizing for weather events.

**Ishita Kapoor:** Ishita, strategy and board observer. I’m here to ask uncomfortable questions and make sure everyone is using the same definition of “fix.”

**Arjun Khanna:** Perfect. That’s enough stakeholders to make any problem look more complicated than it is, which is probably accurate. Let’s start with the business picture. Neha, walk us through the quarter in plain language.

**Neha Malhotra:** In plain language? We missed plan, our cash runway shortened, and our cost base is too high for the current revenue curve. We had **11 closed-won deals** in Q1, but **7** of them were smaller than expected, and **4** slipped from the prior quarter. We also saw a slowdown in collections. Receivables over **60 days** are up to **₹2.6 crore** from **₹1.9 crore** at the end of December. That’s not catastrophic, but it’s enough to make me lose sleep.

**Arjun Khanna:** Great. That’s the kind of sentence that makes leadership meetings feel like dental surgery. Rhea, from sales, what happened?

**Rhea Iyer:** A few things happened at once. First, deal cycles got longer. Our average cycle time went from **48 days to 67 days**. Second, we lost momentum on two enterprise accounts that were expecting custom integrations by mid-March, and those slipped into April. Third, pricing became a bigger issue than we anticipated. Some prospects pushed back hard on the annual contract value, especially when competitors came in with aggressive discounting.

**Maya Iyer:** I’ll add that our campaigns brought traffic, but not enough qualified traffic. We got decent top-of-funnel numbers. The problem is that the leads weren’t moving well after first contact. We generated **2,400 MQLs** in the quarter, but only **420** became SQLs. That conversion rate is lower than we expected, and some of the drop-off is on the handoff between marketing and sales.

**Kabir Sethi:** That’s a nice diplomatic way of saying the handoff is broken.

**Maya Iyer:** Yes, diplomacy is what I do when the internet is watching. But the handoff is a problem. We need to tighten the qualification criteria and stop celebrating traffic like it’s revenue.

**Arjun Khanna:** Noted. Product, engineering, you’ve both been in the hot seat for delivery delays. Vikram, what’s the story?

**Vikram Menon:** The story is that we shipped later than planned. The reporting module slipped by **five weeks**, and the workflow automation release slipped by **three weeks** because of dependency issues and a couple of bugs that kept reappearing in QA. We also underestimated the integration complexity with two customer systems. That hurt. But I want to be clear: it wasn’t just an engineering problem. We got scope changes late, requirements shifted after demos, and support escalations pulled the team into fire-fighting mode.

**Ananya Roy:** That’s true, but we also had too much context switching. In March alone, engineering handled **14 production bugs**, **9 enhancement requests**, and a customer-specific integration issue that should have been handled through a formal change request. We were effectively running sprint planning and incident response at the same time.

**Sandeep Rao:** Support was part of that. Ticket volume was up **23%** quarter over quarter. The team handled it, but barely. We had **six** escalations that needed engineering support, and three of those were for the same root cause: permissions misconfiguration in the enterprise workspace.

**Shalini Bose:** And customer success got caught in the middle because customers don’t care which team owns the bug. They just care that the product failed their workflow. We had two renewals on the edge. One was saved only because we offered a temporary service credit and a dedicated support bridge. The other, the **₹36 lakh** account, churned because the client felt we were too slow on resolution.

**Ishita Kapoor:** That’s useful, but I want us to move beyond symptoms. A bad quarter usually means multiple things are broken at once. So let me ask the uncomfortable question: is the product becoming harder to sell because the market doesn’t want it, or because we’re not executing well enough?

**Rhea Iyer:** Honestly, both. The market is still interested, but expectations are higher. Buyers want stronger enterprise features, better security documentation, and a more mature implementation process. If we come in with a weak delivery story, they notice. A year ago, they would have tolerated rough edges. Not now.

**Maya Iyer:** That’s exactly it. We’re in a tougher market, and our story doesn’t yet compensate for that. Our messaging says “fast, flexible, modern.” The buyer hears “small team, still figuring it out.” We need to bridge that gap.

**Arjun Khanna:** Good. Let’s talk about cash. Neha, what’s the runway after this quarter?

**Neha Malhotra:** If we hold current burn, we have about **6.3 months** of runway. If revenue remains flat and collections stay slow, that could fall to just under **5.5 months**. I’m not saying we’re in immediate danger, but I am saying nobody should go shopping for unnecessary tools or hires.

**Prakash Nair:** Speaking of hires, we froze **four** open roles in February, and that decision was necessary, but it also created pressure in engineering and operations. People are working harder than they should. Morale is not terrible, but it’s not healthy either. Exit interviews are starting to show a pattern: people are tired of ambiguity and rework.

**Ananya Roy:** That tracks. Engineers don’t mind hard work. They mind changing priorities three times in a week and then being asked why velocity looks weak.

**Kabir Sethi:** Operations sees it too. We had one process review on **21 March**, then another on **2 April**, and the same workflow was discussed differently in both meetings because the underlying requirement changed. That creates confusion downstream.

**Arjun Khanna:** So far the theme seems to be: we have a real business, but the machine is noisy and expensive. Ishita, from a board perspective, what are you hearing?

**Ishita Kapoor:** I’m hearing a company that still has market relevance but is losing credibility because execution is inconsistent. The board will not be sympathetic if the explanation is simply “market conditions.” Competitors are facing the same market. Some are doing better. So the question is what we change in the next **90 days** that materially improves the trajectory.

**Arjun Khanna:** Good framing. Let’s identify the pain points. I want no corporate fog. First, sales. Rhea, what’s the most important reason deals are slipping?

**Rhea Iyer:** Three reasons. One, price pressure. Two, product readiness on enterprise integrations. Three, slow legal turnaround on contract redlines. Several deals got delayed because the customer’s procurement team asked for security and implementation details we couldn’t produce quickly enough.

**Maya Iyer:** That’s also a marketing problem indirectly. If we look junior on the website and in our collateral, serious buyers question us earlier. We had one prospect say the site felt “too startup” for the pricing we were asking. That hurts.

**Arjun Khanna:** Fine. Product readiness. Vikram, what can we realistically ship in the next quarter?

**Vikram Menon:** We can stabilize the reporting module, complete the permissions overhaul, and improve the onboarding flow. We should not try to launch three major features at once. The biggest mistake this quarter was trying to push too many parallel efforts. If we concentrate, we can improve reliability and reduce incidents.

**Ananya Roy:** I agree. Engineering needs fewer moving targets. If we freeze one major roadmap stream for a month, we can close a lot of quality gaps. Right now the team is spending too much time reacting instead of building.

**Arjun Khanna:** Which stream gets frozen?

**Vikram Menon:** I’d freeze the experimental analytics features and move them to next quarter. They are nice to have, but they’re not what’s losing us deals. The enterprise buyers care more about stability, permissions, and implementation readiness.

**Rhea Iyer:** Sales agrees. Nobody is losing a deal because we don’t have a flashy chart. They’re losing confidence because we don’t look mature enough or stable enough.

**Maya Iyer:** That means marketing needs to shift too. I want us to stop using generic “innovation” messaging and start using proof-based content. Case studies, implementation timelines, security posture, and actual customer outcomes. We need to sound like a company that has survived contact with reality.

**Sandeep Rao:** Support can contribute to that, but I need one thing from engineering: fewer late-breaking changes without documentation. We had two incidents where the release notes lagged behind the actual deployment. That makes support look clueless when customers ask what changed.

**Ananya Roy:** Fair. That’s on us. We can tighten release notes and change logs. I’ll have engineering set a stricter release communication process starting this week.

**Shalini Bose:** That will help. Customer success also needs better visibility into product issues. Right now, we often hear about bugs from customers before we hear about them internally. That’s not a good look.

**Arjun Khanna:** Okay, so the first broad decision seems obvious: we stop trying to do everything at once. But let’s be more concrete. What are the top priorities for the next 90 days?

**Neha Malhotra:** From finance, I’d say collections, margin, and cost control. If we don’t improve collections, we’ll be pressured on cash. If margin stays low, we can’t afford much experimentation.

**Rhea Iyer:** From sales, enterprise retention and a tighter sales motion. We need to close the existing pipeline faster and stop wasting time on low-probability deals.

**Maya Iyer:** From marketing, better lead quality, better positioning, and a more credible enterprise story.

**Vikram Menon:** From product and engineering, stabilize the platform and finish the permissions and reporting fixes.

**Shalini Bose:** From customer success, renewal protection and better issue escalation.

**Sandeep Rao:** From support, reduce ticket volume and improve first-response consistency.

**Prakash Nair:** From HR, reduce burnout, improve communication, and stop making people feel like every week is a crisis drill.

**Kabir Sethi:** From operations, simplify approvals and remove duplicate process steps.

**Ishita Kapoor:** From the board’s side, I need measurable milestones, not broad intentions. Give me three or four things we can check at the next review and judge progress against.

**Arjun Khanna:** Good. Let’s do that. But before we finalize milestones, I want us to talk about one uncomfortable thing: leadership behavior. We can blame market, product, sales, support, and marketing all day. But if priorities keep shifting from the top, everyone else is forced to react. That’s on us too.

**Neha Malhotra:** Agreed. Several budget decisions were delayed because we kept waiting for consensus on things that should have been decided faster. That affected vendor timelines and slowed execution.

**Kabir Sethi:** And operations kept getting new priorities without clear ranking. Everything was marked urgent, which meant nothing was actually urgent in a practical sense.

**Arjun Khanna:** That’s fair. I’ll own that. We’ve been too loose with priority language. If everything is high priority, the team can’t focus.

**Rhea Iyer:** The sales team would also benefit from more crisp pricing guidance. We spent too much time negotiating exceptions this quarter. Sometimes we won because we discounted aggressively, but that won’t be sustainable if we keep doing it.

**Neha Malhotra:** Exactly. Discounting helped close a few deals, but it also compressed margin and made future pricing harder. We need a more disciplined approach.

**Ishita Kapoor:** That’s the tension. You can’t discount your way out of structural weakness. It buys time, not health.

**Arjun Khanna:** Alright. Then let’s define the response. I propose we split the next quarter into four workstreams: revenue recovery, product stabilization, customer retention, and operating discipline. Each workstream gets one owner and clear targets.

**Maya Iyer:** That works, but marketing needs a direct revenue goal, not a vague awareness goal. I want a measurable contribution to pipeline quality.

**Rhea Iyer:** And sales needs support on messaging. If marketing changes positioning, sales has to have the new story in hand immediately. Otherwise we keep telling customers different versions of the truth.

**Shalini Bose:** Customer success wants a retention dashboard, not just a monthly summary. If there are accounts at risk, we need to see them early.

**Sandeep Rao:** Support wants faster escalation triage. Some issues are getting stuck because nobody wants to own them between teams.

**Vikram Menon:** Engineering wants a lock on the roadmap. No more surprise “can we also just add this” requests without trade-offs.

**Ananya Roy:** And engineering needs a calmer release process. We’re losing time to rushed fixes.

**Prakash Nair:** People also need the company to stop sounding like it’s one bad invoice away from collapse. Morale is affected by tone, not just numbers.

**Arjun Khanna:** Fair. Tone matters. But so do the numbers. Let’s make the numbers specific. Neha, what should we aim for next quarter?

**Neha Malhotra:** I’d propose **₹16.5 crore** revenue as a realistic target, provided collections improve and we stop chasing low-quality deals. Gross margin should recover to at least **44%**. Burn should come down by **15%** from current run rate. And receivables over 60 days should fall below **₹2 crore** by the end of the quarter.

**Rhea Iyer:** Sales can aim for **₹10 crore** in pipeline coverage with a higher qualification bar. But I want fewer, better deals, not just more calls.

**Maya Iyer:** Marketing can improve SQL conversion from **420** to at least **650** if we tighten the funnel and stop broad campaigns that attract the wrong crowd.

**Vikram Menon:** Product can commit to stabilizing the reporting module and permissions release by **30 May 2026**, assuming we freeze experimental analytics for now.

**Sandeep Rao:** Support can reduce average response time by **25%** if we get proper triage and fewer unplanned changes.

**Kabir Sethi:** Operations can simplify approvals in two internal workflows and cut duplicate handoffs. That should improve turnaround time.

**Prakash Nair:** HR will need to address workload and attrition risk. We had **two resignations** in March and **one** in early April. If this continues, it will hit delivery.

**Arjun Khanna:** Good. Let’s tackle some of the objections floating around in the room. One common concern is that we’re talking too much and executing too little. I agree. So I want owners and dates. Rhea, by when can sales give a stricter qualification framework?

**Rhea Iyer:** By **15 April 2026**. I can have sales ops and the reps aligned on qualification criteria, deal stages, and what counts as genuine pipeline.

**Rhea Iyer:** I think we do. The market still wants what we sell. Buyers just want more confidence, better proof, and stronger reliability. We’re not obsolete. We’re just behind where the market expects us to be.
